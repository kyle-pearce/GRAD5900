# Email Style

## Core Characteristics

### Structure & Format
- **TL;DR for longer emails**: Status updates start with executive summary
- **Structured sections**: Uses headers (Milestones, Risks, Escalations) for clarity
- **Very brief for quick updates**: "Just a heads up" style for simple notifications
- **Signature block**: Includes title, team, organization

### Tone & Voice
- **Friendly but professional**: Uses emoji occasionally to add warmth
- **Direct and concise**: Gets to the point quickly
- **Collaborative**: Builds on others' points, acknowledges contributions
- **Confident**: States positions and recommendations clearly
- **Supportive**: Offers specific help and partnership

### Content Patterns
- **Provides evidence**: Backs up contributions with experience or data
- **Offers specific help**: Not just "let me know" — proposes concrete next steps
- **Manages up proactively**: Keeps stakeholders informed before being asked
- **Quantifies when relevant**: Uses concrete numbers to support claims
- **Flags risks clearly**: Dedicated "Risks" section with recommendations
- **Action-oriented**: Ends with clear next steps or offers to contribute

## Differences from Long-Form Writing

### More Conversational
- Occasional emoji for warmth
- Shorter sentences
- More casual phrasing ("just a heads up")
- Less formal structure (except for status updates)

### Still Maintains Core Principles
- BLUF approach (TL;DR)
- Quantification
- Clear structure for complex updates
- Risk transparency
- Action orientation

### Adapted for Medium
- Brevity prioritized over completeness
- Assumes shared context with recipients
- More personal tone
- Faster to read and respond to

## Habits to Reinforce
- TL;DR for any email longer than 3 paragraphs
- Proactive stakeholder updates
- Evidence-based contributions to discussions
- Offering specific help (not just "let me know if I can help")
- Structured sections for status updates
