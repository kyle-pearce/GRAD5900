# Mental Model

## Decision-Making Patterns

### Strategic Alignment Filter (Default Mode)
- When new work arrives, first question: "Does this fit into my existing programs?"
- If yes: Frame it within existing narrative for leadership visibility
- If no: Prepare rationale for declining based on misalignment with current responsibilities
- Focus on portfolio coherence and strategic storytelling

### Research-Driven Pivots
- Investigates before executing (interviews source team, reviews existing docs)
- Willing to change course when research reveals a better approach
- Chooses innovation over duplication
- User-centric: focuses on what actually helps end users

### Delegation & Empowerment
- Actively delegates technical leadership to team members
- Trusts team members with ownership and visibility
- Focuses on enabling others rather than doing everything yourself

### Strategic Program Management
- Provides regular updates on program status across multiple workstreams
- Identifies patterns in program health and engagement
- Plans multi-quarter roadmaps and transition strategies

### Stakeholder Management
- Manages up effectively (provides updates to manager proactively)
- Coordinates across teams and organizations
- Maintains visibility with leadership on strategic initiatives

### Problem-Solving Approach
- Pattern recognition: Identifies systemic issues early
- Proactive: Shares resources and documentation before being asked
- Collaborative: Seeks input and coordinates with partners
- Technology leverage: Comfortable using cutting-edge tools to solve real problems

## Strengths

### What Others Currently Rely On
- **Technical Problem-Solving**: Deep dive into technical problems and figure out solutions
- **Volunteer Mobilization**: Recruit volunteers and organize them around goals
- **Program Creation**: Create goals, plans, and programs for solving problems
- **Mentorship & Development**: Mentors others through career transitions; shares learning resources proactively
- **Resource Sharing & Knowledge Management**: Frequently shares documentation, best practices, and learning resources; creates institutional memory; helps others navigate organizational complexity
- **Communication Clarity**: Uses AI assistants for proofreading and drafting messages; focuses on clarity and brevity; values succinctness and tone
- **Program Management Execution**: Manages complex, multi-stakeholder initiatives; tracks dependencies and risks across workstreams; provides clear status updates and next steps

### Underutilized Strengths (Strategic Leadership)
- **Strategic Leadership Principles**: Applies doctrine from high-stakes leadership environments:
  - **Commander's Intent**: Communicate the *why* so teams can act independently when circumstances change
  - **Mission Clarity**: Define clear objectives and success criteria before execution begins
  - **Second and Third-Order Thinking**: Identify downstream effects of decisions before committing
  - **After Action Reviews**: Systematic reflection after outcomes — what worked, what didn't, what changes next time
  - **Contingency Planning**: Never single-thread critical paths; always have a backup plan
  - **Leading by Example**: Demonstrate the standards you expect from others
  - **Troop Welfare**: Take care of your people first; performance follows trust and morale
- **Gap**: Others see tactical execution strengths but may not see the strategic leadership operating underneath

### Implication for Promotion
- Need to make strategic leadership more visible
- Need artifacts that demonstrate strategic thinking, not just execution
- Connect prior leadership experience to current-level work

## Blind Spots

### Follow-Through on Strategic Decisions
- Can identify the right strategic move and get leadership buy-in
- Documentation and formal handoff steps get squeezed when competing priorities arise
- Result: Decisions sit in limbo — neither fully executed nor abandoned
- Pattern: "Write the doc and close the loop" step gets deprioritized

### Competing Priorities Management
- Takes on complex, high-visibility scope across multiple initiatives
- Can get pulled away from follow-through on other initiatives
- Risk: Backlog builds up on lower-priority items

### Visibility for Own Impact
- Focus on enabling others and delegating — need to ensure own visibility and credit
- Need to be more deliberate about documenting and communicating own contributions
